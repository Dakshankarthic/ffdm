01. PROJECT OVERVIEW
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Working title:
  Detection and Localization of Small AI-Edited Objects in Photographs

Short description:
  Start with an otherwise genuine photograph. A generative AI editor adds,
  removes or replaces a small object or region. Develop and evaluate a detector
  that receives the potentially edited image and identifies whether it contains
  an AI edit and where the suspicious region is located.

Examples:
  - A small globe is removed from a cabinet in a living-room photograph.
  - A bottle or bag is added to a park bench.
  - One small desk object is replaced with another object.
  - One item in a street-market scene is added, removed or replaced.

Core questions:
  Q1. Does this image contain evidence of a localized AI edit?
  Q2. Where is the suspected edited region?

The intended model examines ONE image at inference time. It does not require
the original photograph as a reference input.

The original photograph and known edit information are useful during dataset
construction, supervised training and evaluation. They allow the researcher to
know what was changed and assess the detector's prediction.

Expected research outcome:
  A reproducible study and a tested improvement on a specific weakness in small
  AI-edit detection/localization. A trained system, evaluation report or paper
  is a future deliverable; none exists yet.

04. THE INSTAGRAM EXAMPLE AND PERSPECTIVE EXPLANATION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Reel shared by the user:
  https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfAHPI-yAO7/

Account identified in the user's caption/screenshot:
  himaniac.tech

What was actually available:
  - The user-pasted caption.
  - A screenshot showing railway lines meeting at an orange vanishing point.
  - The full reel was not viewed. Web loading was throttled and browser access
    was denied by a saved permission setting. The record must not imply that
    the complete video or its separate kitchen example was inspected.

Caption's main idea:
  Perspective errors can expose physically inconsistent image structure.

Correct geometric interpretation:
  - Under an ideal perspective camera, straight lines parallel in the same
    world direction project toward a common vanishing point.
  - A room can have several valid vanishing points for different directions.
  - A vanishing point may lie outside the visible picture or at infinity.
  - Two nonparallel image lines usually intersect somewhere. Their intersection
    alone does not establish consistency. Three or more suitable lines allow
    a meaningful common-point consistency check.
  - The difficult step is deciding which observed edges should be parallel in
    3D; merely detecting straight segments does not solve that problem.
  - Lens distortion, panorama stitching, unsuitable edge grouping and uncertain
    measurements can produce apparent errors in genuine photographs.
  - Correct geometry does not prove that an image is authentic.

Research relevance:
  Farid explored perspective consistency in 2022. CVPR 2024 work already used
  lines, perspective fields and object-shadow relationships for detection.
  ControlVP, WACV 2026, improves perspective consistency in generated images.

Current role in this project:
  HISTORICAL / OPTIONAL FEATURE. Geometry is not the confirmed main method.
  It may be evaluated as a complementary signal if experiments justify it.

05. RESEARCH GAP AND NOVELTY BOUNDARY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Broad problem:
  A small manipulated region can be difficult to detect when most of the image
  remains genuine. The detector should identify evidence relevant to that edit
  and generalize beyond the particular editor used during training.

Working research question:
  How can we reliably detect and localize small AI-edited objects when most of
  the photograph remains unchanged?

More specific candidate question — PROPOSED:
  Can localization become less dependent on generator processing traces outside
  the edited region, while retaining useful detection on unfamiliar editors?

Why this candidate is worth investigating:
  Some editing pipelines reconstruct parts or all of the image through a
  generative decoder. This may leave detectable traces beyond the intended edit.
  A detector can partly rely on these traces instead of evidence specific to
  the changed object or region.

  FUSED (August 2026 preprint) explicitly reports that all methods it evaluated,
  including its own, partly rely on the global generator artifact. DailyBench
  (2026 preprint) reports transfer difficulties for modern whole-image synthesis
  and object-level manipulations. These are literature findings, not results
  obtained by this project.

Existing work already covers:
  - Global real-versus-generated image classification.
  - Partial-manipulation detection and pixel-level localization.
  - Combining semantic and forensic features.
  - Cross-generator testing and compression/resize robustness.
  - Bias-reduced training and confidence calibration.
  - Geometry-based image forensics.
  - Continual adaptation to new generators.

What is NOT a sufficient new contribution by itself:
  - Training a standard classifier and calling the task new.
  - Adding a heatmap without validating localization.
  - Combining familiar feature types without showing a specific benefit.
  - Testing compression or held-out generators without checking prior work.
  - Repeating the background-restoration experiment already studied by FUSED.

What could become a defensible contribution — PROPOSED:
  - A measured improvement on small edits across unseen editing models.
  - A targeted method reducing a documented dependence on unrelated background
    processing, with a controlled comparison showing why it works.
  - Better localization at a controlled false-positive rate on genuine photos.
  - A reproducible dataset/protocol that isolates a distinct, under-tested
    failure, with evidence that existing methods fail for that reason.

No novelty or workshop-acceptance claim has been established. The selected
direction is a research problem; its exact contribution must follow focused
related-work checking and experiments.

06. SYSTEM INPUT, OUTPUTS AND ARCHITECTURE FLOW
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONFIRMED INPUT
  One photograph to inspect, potentially containing a localized AI edit.

DESIRED OUTPUT
  - An image-level assessment of possible AI manipulation.
  - A highlighted suspicious region.

PROPOSED SUPPORTING OUTPUTS
  - A manipulation score, calibrated if presented as confidence.
  - A pixel-level mask or heatmap.
  - A short description such as "suspected edited region near the cabinet".
  - An uncertain result when evidence is insufficient; decision policy is open.

Single-image inference flow — PROPOSED IMPLEMENTATION:

  Inspected image
       |
       v
  Documented preprocessing
       |
       v
  Pretrained feature extractor / existing forensic model
       |
       +--------------------------+
       |                          |
       v                          v
  Image-level score          Regional/pixel-level scores
       |                          |
       v                          v
  Detection decision         Suspected edit mask / heatmap
       |                          |
       +------------+-------------+
                    |
                    v
  Report: possible edit, highlighted region, score and relevant uncertainty

This is a conceptual architecture. No encoder, classifier, segmentation head,
fusion module or training loss has been selected or implemented.

Meaning of "what changed":
  The intended detector identifies WHERE an image appears to have been edited.
  It may describe the visible object or suspicious area. With only the final
  image, it generally cannot reliably recover the exact earlier object or prove
  the complete editing history.

Example:
  Original: cup on a table.
  Edited: bottle in that position.
  Single-image detector: can aim to highlight the bottle region as suspicious.
  Original-versus-edited comparison: can establish cup-to-bottle replacement.

Recovering a precise before/after change is a separate reference-comparison
task, not a requirement of the chosen single-image detector.

07. TRAINING, EVALUATION AND ACTUAL-USE DISTINCTION
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DATA CONSTRUCTION
  The researcher retains the original, creates the edit, records the intended
  mask and editing procedure, and verifies the resulting labels.

SUPERVISED TRAINING — PROPOSED
  Typical inputs/targets: genuine and edited images, image labels and edit masks.
  Originals can help construct paired data and controlled experiments. An
  original reference is not inherently required as a second model input.

EVALUATION
  The detector receives one held-out image.
  A separate evaluator uses its hidden label/mask to score the predictions.
  Ground truth must not be supplied to the detector as an inference input.

ACTUAL USE
  The user supplies only the photograph to inspect.
  The detector produces the image assessment and suspicious-region prediction.

Comparison:

  Stage              Model sees                     Researcher/evaluator keeps
  -----------------  -----------------------------  ---------------------------
  Training           Training image                 Label/mask for supervision
  Held-out testing   One test image                  Hidden true label/mask
  Actual use         One unknown image               Original usually unavailable

Mask convention — PROPOSED:
  White / 255: intended edited region.
  Black / 0: region outside the intended edit.

Important distinction:
  A requested edit mask is not automatically an exact map of all pixels the
  editor modified. Some editors resize, recompress, regenerate or alter areas
  outside the selection. Record both the requested selection and the observed
  editing behavior. Define whether the ground truth represents the requested
  semantic edit, generator-processed pixels or another precisely stated target.

11. PROPOSED BASELINE AND DEVELOPMENT APPROACH
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

No final method has been selected. Begin with released code and checkpoints
where feasible, then establish which failure the project will improve.

Relevant baseline candidates:
  - DeCLIP: a published approach for localizing generated image modifications.
  - FUSED: a recent joint inpainting detection/localization approach.
  - DiffusionPrint: forensic features intended to improve inpainting localization.
  - A global image detector: useful as a comparison showing how whole-image
    detection differs from regional localization.

These candidates were identified from research. They have not been installed,
run or ranked on this project's images. Check code availability, model/data
licenses, dependencies and compute requirements before selecting one.

Suggested sequence:
  A. Create and inspect the first controlled edited sample.
  B. Select an existing annotated manipulation benchmark for meaningful testing.
  C. Run a released baseline on genuine and edited examples.
  D. Examine failures, especially small edit regions.
  E. Select one testable failure mechanism.
  F. Propose one targeted method or training intervention.
  G. Compare it with baseline and simple alternatives using the same data.

Potential failure mechanisms to inspect, not established project findings:
  - Local evidence overwhelmed by the authentic background.
  - Reliance on global generator reconstruction artifacts.
  - Performance loss on an unfamiliar editor.
  - Poor localization after compression or resizing.
  - False alarms caused by genuine photographic processing.

Potential experiment on background artifacts — PROPOSED:
  Compare a generator's output with a controlled version retaining original
  pixels outside the edit region. Keep edit content fixed as far as possible.
  Examine both detection and localization. Check compositing boundaries and
  annotation definitions to avoid introducing new shortcuts.

This experiment is already present in related work. A contribution would need
to explain and reduce a remaining weakness, or extend evaluation in a clearly
distinct and justified way.

12. EVALUATION PLAN
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Status: PROPOSED. No numerical results or targets have been measured.

Two tasks must be assessed separately:

  IMAGE-LEVEL DETECTION
    Does the system distinguish genuine photos from photos with local AI edits?

  REGION-LEVEL LOCALIZATION
    How well does its predicted suspicious region match the defined edit mask?

Candidate detection measures:
  - AUROC: separation across decision thresholds.
  - Precision/recall: behavior at a selected operational threshold.
  - False-positive rate on genuine photos.
  - Recall at a preset false-positive rate.

Candidate localization measures:
  - Intersection-over-Union (IoU).
  - Pixel-level F1/Dice.
  - Per-image mask quality and qualitative overlays.

Choose thresholds using training/validation information, not hidden test labels.
State the thresholding and mask-postprocessing rules before reporting results.

Suggested result breakdowns:
  - Small versus larger edited regions, using documented area bins.
  - Addition versus removal versus replacement.
  - Known versus held-out editing model.
  - Untouched outputs versus defined compression/resize conditions.
  - Genuine-photo source/camera collections where available.

Useful controls:
  - Genuine images processed through equivalent export/compression steps.
  - Equal preprocessing policies for genuine and manipulated images.
  - Source-grouped splits with no near-duplicate leakage.
  - A simple baseline and ablation isolating each proposed intervention.
  - Confidence intervals when sample counts support meaningful estimates.

Fair comparison:
  Record training data, test data, checkpoint/version, compute, preprocessing,
  image sizes and thresholds. Mark any result taken from a paper separately
  from results reproduced in this workspace.

Claims to avoid:
  - "100% accurate" without a carefully defined and adequate test population.
  - Treating five demonstrations as evidence of general performance.
  - Calling an uncalibrated model score a proven probability of manipulation.
  - Claiming that a heatmap reconstructs the exact original image.

  Working design name: HiRes-FUSED / Spatially-Anchored Multi-Scale

  pred_score: (B,1), manipulation score (not calibrated by default).
  pred_3class_logits: (B,3,H,W), per-pixel class logits.
  pred_mask: (B,1,H,W), local-edit probability map.
