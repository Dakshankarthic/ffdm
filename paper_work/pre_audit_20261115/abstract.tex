\begin{abstract}
Modern diffusion-based generative inpainting can seamlessly insert, replace, or remove localized visual content while preserving the global semantic coherence of a photograph. Detecting and localizing such subtle manipulations poses a fundamental challenge for visual forensics: standard whole-image downsampling erases high-frequency boundary seams and diffusion-specific noise discrepancies, while isolated native-crop inspection lacks the global context required to differentiate genuine textured surfaces from synthetic modifications. Furthermore, decoupled crop supervision frequently assigns positive image-level labels to clean background patches, artificially inflating false alarm rates on pristine imagery. This paper presents \textbf{FRAME} (\textbf{F}orensic \textbf{R}esidual \textbf{A}ttention with \textbf{M}ulti-scale \textbf{E}vidence), an end-to-end framework for joint single-image inpainting detection and pixel-level localization. FRAME resolves the scale-context dichotomy through a dual-view architecture that simultaneously processes a resized global view for macroscopic scene context and overlapping native-resolution sliding tiles at $1:1$ pixel scale to preserve microscopic artifacts. Features are extracted via a Tri-Stream encoder combining hierarchical representations from ConvNeXt-Base, self-supervised foundation tokens from frozen DINOv2-Base, and a 60-channel noise residual pyramid constructed from fixed Spatial Rich Model (SRM) and learned Bayar constrained convolutions. To selectively elevate forensic anomalies without inducing semantic false alarms, a Multi-Head Cross-Attention Decoder (MH-CAD) employs high-pass residual tokens as queries to cross-attend to dense semantic keys and values across multiple pyramid stages. Downstream, we formulate a joint dual-view loss function integrating balanced Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE), soft Dice segmentation, and clean-image false-positive suppression penalties, widening the genuine-versus-edited decision separation gap by $4.3\times$. Evaluated on the multi-generator OpenSDID benchmark across five diffusion families (Stable Diffusion 1.5, 2.1, 3, SDXL, and FLUX.1), FRAME achieves \textbf{91.20\% image accuracy}, \textbf{92.60\% balanced accuracy}, \textbf{93.40\% AUROC}, and \textbf{47.30\% mean localization IoU}, outperforming leading state-of-the-art architectures including FUSED ($89.50\%$ Acc, $44.50\%$ IoU), MaskCLIP ($82.00\%$ Acc, $42.70\%$ IoU), and DeCLIP ($73.10\%$ Acc, $25.20\%$ IoU), while advancing tiny-edit localization ($<2\%$ area) to $13.60\%$ IoU.
\end{abstract}
